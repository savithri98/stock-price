Stock Market Basics

What is the Stock Market?

The stock market is a collection of exchanges where shares of publicly traded companies are bought and sold. It provides companies with access to capital and investors with ownership in companies and the potential for returns.

When you buy a company's stock, you're purchasing a small piece of that company, called a share. Investors profit when the companies they invest in grow and perform well, which increases the value of their shares.

How Stock Exchanges Work

Stock exchanges like the NYSE (New York Stock Exchange) and NASDAQ provide the infrastructure for trading stocks. They match buyers with sellers and ensure fair and transparent transactions through:

  • Order Matching: Connecting buy and sell orders at agreed prices
  • Price Discovery: Determining stock prices through supply and demand
  • Liquidity Provision: Ensuring there are enough buyers and sellers
  • Transaction Settlement: Completing the exchange of shares and money

Market Analysis Techniques

Fundamental Analysis

Fundamental analysis involves evaluating a company's financial health and intrinsic value by examining related economic, financial, and other qualitative and quantitative factors.

Key metrics used in fundamental analysis include:

  • Earnings Per Share (EPS): Company profit divided by outstanding shares
  • Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio: Stock price divided by earnings per share
  • Debt-to-Equity Ratio: Company debt compared to shareholder equity
  • Return on Equity (ROE): How effectively management uses investor money

Technical Analysis

Technical analysis focuses on statistical trends gathered from trading activity, such as price movement and volume. Unlike fundamental analysts, technical analysts don't attempt to measure a security's intrinsic value.

Common technical analysis tools include:

  1. Moving Averages: Smooth out price data to identify trends
  2. Relative Strength Index (RSI): Measures speed and change of price movements
  3. Bollinger Bands: Volatility bands placed above and below a moving average
  4. MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence): Shows relationship between two moving averages

Common Stock Price Patterns

Technical analysts study price patterns to predict future market movements. These patterns form when security prices chart a recognizable shape, which can signal whether prices will continue in their current direction or reverse.

Technical Chart Patterns
Visualization of common technical analysis chart patterns. Click the dots above to switch between patterns.

Head and Shoulders

A reversal pattern that predicts a bullish-to-bearish trend reversal. It consists of three peaks, with the middle peak (head) being the highest and the two outside peaks (shoulders) being lower and roughly equal.

Cup and Handle

A bullish continuation pattern where the price initially drops then rises to form a "U" shape (the cup), followed by a small downward drift (the handle) before breaking out to continue the upward trend.

Double Top/Bottom

Reversal patterns. A double top (M shape) signals a bullish-to-bearish reversal, while a double bottom (W shape) signals a bearish-to-bullish reversal. Both patterns show two failed attempts to break through a price level.

LSTM Neural Networks for Stock Prediction
